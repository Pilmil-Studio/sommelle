Sleep diary: printable, with sleep efficiency

Published on 26 November 2026. This is not medical advice.

Writing down your nights for one or two weeks is the first step of cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), and it is often what a sleep doctor asks for. It shows your nights as they are, not as you remember them after a bad week.

How to fill it in

  • Every morning, within an hour of getting up, for the night just gone.
  • Estimate durations, do not time them. Checking the clock at night increases worry and the time it takes to fall asleep: an estimate is enough.
  • Write down bad nights too, and nights with a sleeping pill or alcohol: the whole picture matters.
  • Change nothing in the first week. Observe first, act later.

Work out the efficiency of a night

Sleep efficiency is the share of time in bed spent asleep. The calculation runs on your device; nothing is sent.

A single night says little: the weekly average is what counts. Time in bed before lights out or after waking counts as time in bed, not as sleep. In CBT-I, low efficiency over a week is used to adjust time in bed, but cutting that time is done within a supervised programme, with precautions: not something to do alone on the strength of a calculation. When to see a doctor

Talk to your doctor, diary in hand, if:

  • you are told you snore loudly with pauses in your breathing, or you are very sleepy during the day, especially when driving;
  • the trouble has lasted more than three months and weighs on your days;
  • you take a sleeping pill or sedative in the evening, have an illness that lack of sleep makes worse, or are pregnant;
  • you have unusual behaviour during sleep (sleepwalking, night terrors).

If you are struggling or having thoughts of suicide, contact your local emergency or crisis line (in France, 3114, free, day and night).
