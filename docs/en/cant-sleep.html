When you cannot sleep: what to do, right now

Published on 26 November 2026. This is not medical advice.

Everyone has a bad night, and it is not dangerous. What keeps insomnia going is mostly what we do to fight it: lying in bed waiting, checking the time, telling ourselves the day is ruined. Here is what cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), the first treatment offered for adult insomnia, recommends.

Tonight

  1. Do not look at the time. Turn the clock around, put the phone face down. Watching the clock increases worry and the time it takes to fall asleep.
  2. If you have been awake for about twenty minutes, estimated without checking, get up. Lying in bed waiting for sleep pushes it away: the bed ends up linked to being awake.
  3. Go to another room, with soft light, and do something calm: read a paper book, listen to quiet music, fold laundry. Away from screens.
  4. Go back to bed when sleep comes back: heavy eyelids, yawning. If you still cannot sleep, do it again. Having to repeat it several times at first is normal.
  5. In the morning, get up at your usual time, even after a bad night. Sleeping in shifts the next night's sleep.

When thoughts keep spinning

  • Set your worries down early in the evening. Take ten minutes, well before bed, to write what is on your mind and, next to it, the next small step, or “nothing to do tonight”. Put the sheet away. If a worry comes back in bed, it is written down: you will deal with it tomorrow.
  • Soften the thoughts that add pressure. “If I don't sleep, tomorrow will be a disaster”: in fact, people often cope better than they fear after a bad night. A short night does not need to be “made up”.
  • Do not try to sleep. Effort wakes you up. The goal of the night is to rest calmly; sleep comes when you stop chasing it.

During the day and in the evening

  • A fixed wake-up time, seven days a week: the most important cue for your body clock.
  • Daylight in the morning, outdoors if you can.
  • Coffee, tea and cola: stop several hours before bed. A synthesis of 24 studies estimates coffee should be taken at least 8 to 9 hours before.
  • No naps, or very short ones early in the afternoon.
  • A calm hour before bed, screen-free if possible.
  • Bed is for sleep (and intimacy), not for work, series or scrolling.

When to see a doctor

If the trouble comes back several nights a week for more than three months and weighs on your days, if you are told you snore loudly with pauses in your breathing, or if you are sleepy during the day, especially when driving: talk to your doctor. Record your nights for a week or two beforehand with our printable sleep diary.

If you are struggling or having thoughts of suicide, contact your local emergency or crisis line (in France, 3114, free, day and night).
